Charitable giving is a powerful force for good in the world and giving well is a duty I take with a deep seriousness. I first learned about [Effective Altruism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_altruism) in 2015 and was immediately enamored with the clear message: donate to the most effective charities, who can prove the value they provide per dollar, even if you will never see the personal impact of that money. Although I have not taken the Giving What We Can [10% Pledge](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/pledge), I began donating primarily towards effective charities - especially those focused on prevention of malaria in sub-Saharan Africa. Though hard to comprehend on an emotional level, since I began donating in this way and based on [GiveWell's](https://www.givewell.org/) estimates, I have helped ~50 children currently living in sub-Saharan Africa who would have otherwise died from easily preventable disease.

Then in 2018, I read one of the the most important books I've encountered, James C. Scott's [Seeing Like a State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeing_Like_a_State) (see [this blog post](https://samzdat.com/2017/05/22/man-as-a-rationalist-animal/) for a great, if dense, synopsis). Effective Altruism is an approach to bring legibility (in Scott's terminology) to charitable giving; it builds and then evaluates against measurements, which advantages the quantities that are *measurable*. These are important quantities to be sure! For example, EA charity evaluators often use QALYs (Quality Adjusted Life Years) saved as one axis measured. This is an attempt to quantify the number of quality years of life an intervention is reasonably expected to produce and helps bring legibility to the common sense idea that adding a few poor years to the end of the life of an elderly person is worth less than adding many great years to a child's life.

However, there are a lot of qualities that are immeasurable, and Scott would caution us against believing the legibility we've artifically built actually quantifies the underlying territory (or whether we're just making the charity version of [Brasilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bras%C3%ADlia)). It's not necessarily that GiveWell's numbers are inaccurate - averting malaria deaths is a real and extremely important thing to count. Instead it's the instict that a legible plan can replace local, illegible knowledge that ensure complex systems work; something he calls *metis*. After reading Seeing Like a State, my belief began to grow that legible giving is not the only effective way to do charity (even if other ways are not as legible and are more grounded in *metis*).

Instead of attempting to unify these two opposing viewpoints into a grand theory of how to do the most good - I took the more expiditious route of explicitly splitting my charitable giving, which means balancing global impact through EA charities with local community support. Global/EA causes are chosen for their proven cost-effectiveness (often measured in lives saved per dollar), while local charities are chosen for their direct community impact and the value of my own local knowledge in addressing issues. By splitting funds between these, I aim to do the most good per dollar globally and also strengthen my local community, leveraging the insight that people closest to the ground often know best how to help their neighbors. Local giving also lets me see the effects first-hand and avoids some unintended consequences that can occur in top-down aid efforts. This is a hard-won compromise; every dollar I give to a local effort is a dollar not going to EA, which means potentially a dollar that doesn't save a child from an ugly death. I often revisit this but also reaffirm that I can be guided by my own *metis*, even against problems that are illegible.

## Updated 2025 List

## Global Causes
* **Malaria Consortium**
	* Focus: Preventing malaria in sub-Saharan Africa, especially for children. 
	* Impact: Supports seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC) programs, one of the most cost-effective health interventions. GiveWell estimates around $4,000 per life saved through this program. The Malaria Consortium’s work is backed by strong evidence (multiple trials show SMC significantly reduces child mortality) and robust monitoring.
* **Helen Keller International** 
	* Focus: Combating vitamin A deficiency and other causes of preventable blindness and malnutrition, mainly in Africa.
	* Impact: Runs vitamin A supplementation programs for children under 5, which improves immune health and reduces child mortality. GiveWell estimates about $3,500 per life saved via HKI’s vitamin A programs. HKI has a long track record and strong evidence base for its micronutrient and eye health interventions.
* **GiveDirectly** 
	* Focus: Direct cash transfers to extremely low-income households (primarily in East Africa, and some programs in other regions).
	* Impact: Gives money directly to people living in poverty, allowing them to spend on their most pressing needs. This approach has overwhelming evidence of positive impacts – recipients tend to invest in nutrition, healthcare, education, and small businesses, leading to higher incomes and assets. Studies also show each $1 delivered can generate about $1.50–$2.50 in local economic activity as communities prosper and allows for donations to act more like local donations since the local community directs the funds

## Local Community Causes
* **Clark Fork Coalition** - Conservation of the Clark Fork River watershed in Western Montana
* **Missoula Food Bank & Community Center** - Fighting hunger and food insecurity in Missoula County. They use a a client-choice model (where families can select food)
* **Garden City Harvest** - Local agriculture, community gardens, and hunger relief in Missoula. Garden City Harvest runs 22 community gardens and small urban farms across Missoula
* **Lorilee Evans-Lynn Memorial Scholarship Endowment** - Supporting local youth in the creative arts, gives a $1500 scholarship to a graduating senior, a memorial scholarship for a particularly impactful teacher I had in high school
* **Missoula Children’s Theatre**. Youth development through performing arts, locally and globally.
* **Five Valleys Land Trust**. Land conservation in Western Montana, since 1972, Five Valleys has helped conserve over 97,000 acres of western Montana’s open land
* **Home ReSource**. Sustainability, waste reduction, and community reuse in Missoula.
* **Wheels on the Bus Ceramic Studio**. Arts access for youth in Missoula.
* **Downtown Women’s Center (Los Angeles)** - DWC is the only organization in LA exclusively serving women experiencing homelessness. It provides safe housing and comprehensive supportive services – including health and wellness care, job training through its social enterprise, and advocacy
